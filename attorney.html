Background

USPTO Patent Examiner Experience

From June 2022 to October 2025, I served as a patent examiner at the United States Patent and Trademark Office in Art Units 2498 and 2496. These art units handle some of the most technically complex patent applications in:

  • Artificial intelligence and machine learning
  • Cryptography and blockchain technology
  • Cybersecurity systems
  • Computer security and access control
  • Data encryption and secure communications

During my time at the USPTO, I examined hundreds of patent applications. I learned firsthand what makes applications succeed or fail, which technical details matter to examiners, and how to navigate the most common rejection grounds including abstract idea rejections under Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank and obviousness rejections under 35 U.S.C. § 103.

Technical Background

I understand the technical challenges you face because I've worked through them myself. Whether you're implementing neural networks, designing consensus mechanisms, or building secure systems, I speak your language.

Why I Started QED IP

As a USPTO examiner, I saw brilliant innovations get rejected not because they weren't novel, but because the patent applications were drafted by attorneys who didn't understand the technology or how examiners evaluate these applications.

I started QED IP to bridge that gap. Technical founders need a patent attorney who:

  • Understands the technology deeply enough to capture the true innovation
  • Knows the prior art landscape from examining hundreds of similar applications
  • Can draft claims that survive examination because they know what examiners look for
  • Provides transparent pricing without the BigLaw markup

When you work with QED IP, you work directly with me from start to finish. No handoffs to junior associates. No confusion about who's handling your case. Just straightforward, expert patent protection from someone who has been on both sides of the examination process.
